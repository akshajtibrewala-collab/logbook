Home as an aviation hub

Two directions, mockups only (placeholder data, nothing built). Pick A or B (or tell me what to mix) and I build it with the same checks as the rest of the app. Dark only, real tokens and glass, real tab bar and Add button.

Hub rules (both options)

  • One screen, one story. Where I stand (the hero), what needs me (one status line), where I can go (module tiles). Nothing else competes.
  • Every tile is a real destination. A module appears on Home only when its page exists: no "Soon" tiles, no dead taps. New modules (airports, airlines, photos, trips, journal) join the grid when they are built; the grid grows by one tile each.
  • One live line per tile, at most four words: the one number or word that tells you whether to open it (flights, airports, "All clear", "Go at KSUS"). Detail lives in the module.
  • Same numbers as everywhere. Every figure comes from the same lib function as its module (Logbook total, Travel total, Costs). Presentation only: no new stored data, no new calculation.
  • Role colour is the only accent. Sky dot = pilot, violet dot = passenger, green/amber dot = status. Tiles are neutral; the hero line is the one bright thing.
  • Solid tiles, no glass. Glass stays on the bars; tiles are surfaces at 104px or more, two across on a phone, four across on desktop, and at most eight (the rest stay in More).
  • Word budget unchanged: at most 45 words on the first screen, at most 4 text sizes, one all-caps label (none here). Both options pass today's calm and word checks on paper; the build is checked with the same scripts.
  • Never lies about scope: "Private Pilot" and Logbook are pilot-only; Travel is passenger-only; the map says "airports" for all roles.

Option A: Board

The page is a board of modules. The hero and the status line first, then the tiles, the map as a quiet closing image.

Option B: Map first

Your flying world (the map) is the top of the page; the hero sits on it. The modules are a compact row; the last flights close the page.
